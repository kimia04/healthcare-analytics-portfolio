# Healthcare Spending Trends in the U.S. (1996–2016)

## Overview
Analyzes U.S. healthcare spending trends from 1996 to 2016 across payer types, age groups, sex, care settings, and disease categories to identify long-term expenditure patterns and major cost drivers.

## Dataset
Public healthcare spending data covering payer type, demographic characteristics, care setting, and disease category across a twenty-year period.

## Methods
- Longitudinal trend analysis  
- Data visualization in Python  
- Comparative analysis across payer and disease categories  

## Key Findings
- Spending increases with age across both public and private payers.  
- Cardiovascular disease, musculoskeletal conditions, diabetes, and injury-related care are major cost drivers.

## Key Takeaways
- **Spending by age and payer type:** highlights how public payer costs rise sharply after age 65  
- **Top diseases driving cost:** cardiovascular and musculoskeletal care dominate across all payer types
